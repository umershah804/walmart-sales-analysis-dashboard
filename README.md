# walmart-sales-analysis-dashboard

# Project Overview

This project analyzes Walmart store sales data to identify sales trends, store performance, yearly performance, and the difference between holiday and non-holiday sales.

An interactive dashboard was developed in Microsoft Excel using PivotTables, PivotCharts, KPI cards, and slicers.

The dashboard allows users to interactively analyze sales performance by:

- Year
- Store
- Holiday / Non-Holiday

The main objective of this project is to transform raw retail sales data into meaningful business insights that can support better decision-making.



# Business Objectives

The project focuses on answering the following questions:

- What is the overall sales performance?
- Which year generated the highest sales?
- Which stores are performing best?
- Which stores have lower average weekly sales?
- How do sales change throughout the year?
- Are holiday weeks associated with higher sales?
- Which stores and periods require further investigation?

# Dashboard Features

The dashboard contains six main visualizations.

# 1. Monthly Sales Trend

Shows how sales change across different months and compares sales performance across 2010, 2011, and 2012.

# 2. Total Sales by Year & Store

Compares sales performance across stores and years, helping identify stores with consistently strong or weak performance.

# 3. Total Sales by Store

Ranks the 45 Walmart stores based on their total sales.

# 4. Average Weekly Sales by Store

Compares the typical weekly sales performance of each store.

# 5. Total Sales by Year

Compares overall sales performance across 2010, 2011, and 2012.

# 6. Average Sales: Holiday vs Non-Holiday

Compares average weekly sales during holiday and non-holiday periods.

# 🔍 Key Insights

 1. Strong Overall Sales

The 45 stores generated approximately **$6.74 billion** in total sales during the analyzed period.

This represents a substantial sales volume across the Walmart store network.

---

 2. 2011 Was the Strongest Year

    Based on the yearly analysis:

- 2010: approximately **$2.3B**
- 2011: approximately **$2.4B**
- 2012: approximately **$2.0B**

  2011 recorded the highest total sales.

Sales declined in 2012 compared with 2011, making the decline an important area for further investigation.



 3. Store Performance Varies Significantly

       The Total Sales by Store analysis shows that sales are not evenly distributed across the 45 stores.

    Some stores generate substantially higher sales than others.

    This indicates that store-level factors may have an important influence on sales performance.



4. Holiday Weeks Have Higher Average Sales

   Average weekly sales were approximately:

 - **Non-Holiday:** $1.041M
 - **Holiday:** $1.123M

Holiday weeks therefore had approximately **7.9% higher average weekly sales** than non-holiday weeks.

This indicates that holiday periods represent important sales opportunities.

 Note: This analysis identifies an association between holiday periods and sales. It does not prove that holidays alone caused the increase.



 5. Sales Change Throughout the Year

The monthly sales trend shows noticeable fluctuations throughout the year.

This indicates that customer demand is not constant and that seasonal patterns may influence Walmart sales.



 6. Store-Level Analysis Is Important

Overall company sales can hide significant differences between individual stores.

Analyzing stores separately helps management identify:

- High-performing stores
- Low-performing stores
- Stores requiring further investigation
- Opportunities to replicate successful strategies



# Business Recommendations

 1. Study High-Performing Stores

Management should investigate why certain stores consistently generate higher sales.

Possible factors include:

- Product availability
- Store location
- Customer demographics
- Promotions
- Inventory management
- Staffing
- Store size

Successful strategies could potentially be applied to lower-performing stores.



 2. Develop Improvement Plans for Low-Performing Stores

Stores with consistently low sales should be investigated individually rather than applying one strategy to every location.

Management should analyze:

- Local customer demand
- Product assortment
- Pricing
- Competitor activity
- Inventory availability
- Promotional effectiveness



 3. Prepare for Holiday Demand

Since holiday weeks show approximately **7.9% higher average weekly sales**, Walmart should prepare for increased demand.

Recommended actions include:

- Increase inventory before major holidays.
- Increase staffing during peak periods.
- Prepare promotional campaigns earlier.
- Monitor stock-outs.
- Use previous holiday sales to improve demand forecasting.


 4. Investigate the 2012 Sales Decline

Since 2012 sales were lower than 2011, further analysis should determine why sales declined.

Potential factors to investigate include:

- Store-level performance
- Fuel prices
- Unemployment
- CPI
- Temperature
- Monthly trends
- Holiday periods

This can help determine whether the decline was concentrated in specific stores or occurred across the entire network.



 5. Use Sales Forecasting

Historical monthly and weekly sales data can be used to forecast future demand.

Forecasting could help Walmart improve:

- Inventory planning
- Staffing
- Purchasing
- Promotions
- Store operations



 6. Monitor Store KPIs Regularly

Management should continuously monitor store-level KPIs such as:

- Total Sales
- Average Weekly Sales
- Sales Growth %
- Store Ranking
- Holiday Sales
- Non-Holiday Sales
- Monthly Sales
